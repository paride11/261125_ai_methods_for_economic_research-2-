# Tariffs and the Secondhand Shift 📦 

Research project on how the 2025 U.S. tariff package may affect consumer substitution between new and used goods.

The project studies whether tariff-driven price increases are passed through differently to **new** versus **used/refurbished** goods, and whether consumers respond by shifting demand toward second-hand markets. The analysis focuses on the United States, with Canada and the United Kingdom used as donor/control countries. ## Project Overview

The research focuses on three product categories:

- Fast-fashion clothing
- Portable consumer electronics, such as smartphones and laptops
- Passenger cars

The empirical strategy combines price data, platform listings, and behavioral search indicators to test two main hypotheses:

**H1 — Pass-through asymmetry**  
New goods experience larger post-tariff price increases than comparable used or refurbished goods.

**H2 — Substitution toward used goods**  
Consumers increase their use of second-hand markets after tariff-related price increases, especially for durable categories such as cars and electronics.

## Methodology

The project uses:

- Hedonic machine learning price indices for used goods
- Official new-goods price indices
- Event-study regressions around the tariff implementation period
- Difference-in-differences event studies comparing the U.S. with donor countries
- Google Trends-based secondhand demand and supply indices

## Key Takeaway

If the hypotheses are supported, the project shows that second-hand markets can partly cushion consumers from trade shocks and that quality-adjusted second-hand price indices can complement official price statistics.
